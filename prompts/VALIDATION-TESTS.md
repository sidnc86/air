## Test 2: Case Formulation from Narrative

### Input Case Narrative (Synthesized from Marbella Materials)

> "I'm a blind resident in a new residential tower in Pune. The building was completed in 2023 (phase 1). The main problems are:
> 
> 1. **Lift control buttons:** The touch panels don't have Braille labels and I can't feel which button is which. This makes the lift unusable for me.
> 
> 2. **Accessible routes:** The building has common amenities (clubhouse, library, swimming area) but the only routes there are stairs—there are no ramps or accessible alternatives. I'm essentially locked out of these shared spaces.
> 
> 3. **Wayfinding:** The building doesn't have tactile guiding lines or warning indicators on common paths. I've had several near-misses with obstacles.
> 
> I've taken photos of the lift panels and the stairs. I've documented the building addresses and the phases. The building is registered under RERA. I want to file a complaint but I don't know how to structure it or what legal sections to reference. I need help organizing this into a proper complaint."

## Accessibility Issues

### Issue 1: Lift Control Panel Accessibility

**Description:** Touch panels on residential lift lack Braille labels and tactile feedback, making lift unusable for blind residents

**Technical Facts:**
- Lift type: Residential (automated touch panel)
- Missing features: Braille labels, tactile markings
- User impact: Cannot independently identify or press buttons

**Applicable Standards:**
- RPWD Act 2016, Section 40 (building accessibility), Section 41 (standards)
- NBC 2016, Section 13, Annex B (lift requirements)
  - Control buttons must be at 900–1200 mm height
  - Braille and tactile labeling required
- MOHUA Guidelines 2021 (Braille signage standards)

**Evidence Inventory:**
- ✅ Available: Photos of lift control panel
- ❌ Missing: Measurement of panel height and button size
- ❌ Missing: NBC Section 13 technical specification reference
- ❌ Missing: Expert accessibility audit confirming non-compliance

**Applicable Relief:**
- RERA Section 36: Specific performance (retrofit lift with accessible controls)
- RERA Section 36: Compensation for accessibility defect
- RPWD Act Section 42: Penalties for building accessibility non-compliance

### Issue 2: Accessible Routes to Common Amenities

**Description:** Routes to clubhouse, library, and swimming area rely only on stairs; no ramps or accessible alternatives provided

**Technical Facts:**
- Amenities affected: Clubhouse, library, swimming pool area
- Current access: Stairs only (no alternative routes)
- User impact: Stairs are not accessible for wheelchair users, blind residents with gait difficulties

**Applicable Standards:**
- RPWD Act 2016, Section 40 (building common areas accessibility), Section 41 (standards)
- NBC 2016, Section 13 (ramp specifications, accessible routes)
  - Ramps: maximum slope 1:12 (8.33%)
  - Width: minimum 1200 mm
  - Handrails on both sides for slopes > 1:20
- MOHUA Guidelines 2021 (accessible route requirements)

**Evidence Inventory:**
- ✅ Available: Photos of stairs and current routes
- ❌ Missing: Measurement of stair slopes and widths
- ❌ Missing: Building plan showing accessibility route design
- ❌ Missing: Documentation of how many residents are affected

**Applicable Relief:**
- RERA Section 36: Specific performance (design and construct accessible routes, ramps)
- RERA Section 36: Compensation for common area access defect
- RPWD Act Section 42: Penalties for common area accessibility non-compliance

### Issue 3: Wayfinding and Tactile Guiding Features

**Description:** Common paths lack tactile guiding lines and warning indicators; safety and navigation barriers for blind residents

**Technical Facts:**
- Areas affected: Common corridors, pathways
- Missing features: Tactile paving, guiding lines, warning indicators

**Applicable Standards:**
- RPWD Act 2016, Section 41 (accessibility standards)

**Applicable Relief:**
- RERA Section 36: Specific performance (install tactile guiding and warning systems)
- RERA Section 36: Compensation for navigation defect
- RPWD Act Section 42: Penalties for accessibility non-compliance

## Evidence Summary

**Collected Evidence:**
- Photos of lift control panel (3+ images)
- Photos of common stairs and access routes
- Photos of common corridors and paths
- Documented impact on resident accessibility

**Evidence Gaps (Priority for Gathering):**
1. **Highest Priority:** Measurements (lift panel height, stair dimensions, ramp slopes)
   - Why: NBC Section 13 defines specific measurements; measurements prove non-compliance
2. **Medium Priority:** Building plan and RERA registration documents
   - Why: Establishes building scope and RERA jurisdiction
3. **Medium Priority:** Professional accessibility audit
   - Why: Expert report strengthens claim and provides technical standards verification
4. **Lower Priority:** Affected resident documentation
   - Why: Shows impact beyond one person; strengthens equity argument

## Next Steps for Complainant

**Before Filing RERA Complaint:**
1. Obtain building RERA registration copy (verify registration and project scope)
2. Measure lift panel height and button size (reference NBC 2016 Section 13)
3. Measure or calculate stair slopes and route dimensions
4. Organize photos with dates and location descriptions
5. Consider professional accessibility audit (builds credibility)

**Filing RERA Complaint:**
1. File complaint under RERA Section 11 (5-year window from possession)
2. Attach evidence package: photos, measurements, RERA registration copy
3. Reference applicable sections: RPWD Act 40/41, NBC 2016 Section 13, MOHUA Guidelines
4. Request specific relief: Lift retrofit + accessible routes + wayfinding installation
5. Request compensation for accessibility defect (under RERA Section 36)

**Alternative/Parallel Action:**
1. File accessibility complaint with State Disability Commissioner (RPWD Act authority)
2. Request inspection by municipal building authority (NBC compliance)
3. Engage with accessibility advocacy organizations for precedent support

## Usage Recommendations

### For Case Contributors

**Use case-formulation.md when:**
- You have accessibility barriers to document
- You want to structure your narrative before consulting a lawyer
- You need to organize evidence and identify what's missing
- You're preparing material for knowledge base contribution

**Use verify-legal-claims.md when:**
- You have a structured case and want to validate claims against law
- You're checking if you have adequate evidence before filing
- You want to identify applicable relief sections
- You need to determine next steps (gather evidence, file, consult lawyer)

### For Advocacy Organizations

**Use both prompts sequentially:**
1. **Formulate** a case narrative from a community member
2. **Verify** the structured claims against legal mandates
3. **Refine** based on gaps and recommendations
