## Comparative Lift-Control Example: U.S. ADA Standards

This is a U.S.-specific example, not an Indian requirement. The U.S. Access
Board's [2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/)
state that Braille shall be contracted (Grade 2) in Section 703.3. The elevator
control table in Section 407.4.7.1.3 shows the following labels, contraction
notation, and cell counts:

| Control | Label in ADA table | Contraction shown | Unicode Braille (UEB cells; no capitals) | Cells |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency stop | STOP | ST | ⠌⠕⠏ | 3 |
| Alarm | ALARM | AR | ⠁⠇⠜⠍ | 4 |
| Door open | OPEN | EN | ⠕⠏⠢ | 3 |
| Door close | CLOSE | None shown | ⠉⠇⠕⠎⠑ | 5 |
| Main entry floor | MAIN | IN | ⠍⠁⠔ | 3 |
| Phone | PHONE | ONE | ⠏⠓⠐⠕ | 4 |

The Unicode column applies the corresponding English UEB contraction cells
from the [ICEB Rules of Unified English Braille, Third Edition (2024), Section
10](https://iceb.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Rules-of-Unified-English-Braille-2024.pdf):
ST, AR, EN, IN, and the two-cell ONE contraction. These are UEB letter and
contraction cells, not Unicode transcriptions printed by the ADA standard.
Capital indicators are omitted to align with the ADA table's listed cell
counts; this is not a complete production transcription. Do not apply these
patterns to Indian lift panels without verifying the applicable Indian code,
language, grade, capitalization conventions, and transcription with an
authoritative source and qualified Braille reviewer. Indian requirements
remain unverified in this reference. Checked 2026-11-04.
