# Mandate Precedence Map

This file explains which accessibility instruments bind a building project in India, in what order they apply, and how the files in this repository relate to each other. It is the project's own reading of the documents, prepared 2026-11-13. It is not legal advice, and items marked **Needs legal review** should be confirmed by a lawyer before anyone relies on them.

---

## The order of authority

Higher levels prevail over lower ones where they conflict. A lower level can still add stricter requirements, as long as it does not contradict a higher one.

| Level | Instrument | In this repository | Kind | How it becomes binding |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 | [`national/03-RPWD-Act-2016.md`](national/03-RPWD-Act-2016.md) | Central statute | Binding in itself |
| 2 | RPwD Rules, 2017 (Rule 15 on accessibility standards) | [`national/04-RPWD-Rules-2017.md`](national/04-RPWD-Rules-2017.md) | Rules made under Sections 40 and 100 of the Act | Binding in itself. See the court challenge noted below |
| 3 | Standards that Rule 15(1)(a) prescribes "for public buildings": Harmonised Guidelines 2016 until 4 June 2023, Harmonised Guidelines 2021 from 5 June 2023 | [`national/05-MoUD-Harmonised-Guidelines-2016.md`](national/05-MoUD-Harmonised-Guidelines-2016.md); [`national/01-MOHUA-building-guidelines-2021.md`](national/01-MOHUA-building-guidelines-2021.md) | Government guidelines | Binding through Rule 15(1)(a), for public buildings |
| 4 | State planning law and building regulations (Maharashtra: MRTP Act, 1966 and UDCPR 2020) | [`mh/01-UDCPR-2016-Maharashtra.md`](mh/01-UDCPR-2016-Maharashtra.md) | State law and regulations | Binding for building permission in the state |
| 5 | Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 | [`mh/02-Real-Estate-Act-2016-Sections-11-15.md`](mh/02-Real-Estate-Act-2016-Sections-11-15.md) | Central statute | Binds the promoter to the sanctioned plans, specifications and what was advertised or agreed |
| 6 | BIS codes and standards: NBC 2016, NBCS 2026, IS 4963 : 2025, IS 15330 : 2020 | [`national/02-NBC-2016-Accessibility-Clause-Map.md`](national/02-NBC-2016-Accessibility-Clause-Map.md); others not yet mapped | Voluntary national standards | Only where a law, regulation, sanction condition or contract adopts them. Otherwise persuasive evidence of good practice |

RERA sits at level 5 because it does not set accessibility standards of its own. It enforces whatever the project was sanctioned with and whatever the promoter promised, so its force in an accessibility complaint depends on levels 1 to 4 and on the project documents.

## What each level says, in brief

**RPwD Act, 2016.** Section 40 requires the Central Government to make rules setting accessibility standards. Section 44 then makes those rules a condition of building permission for "any structure" and of the completion certificate and occupation. Section 2(i) and 2(v) bring private companies within "establishment", and Section 2(x) names housing as a public facility and service. Section 96 says the Act adds to other laws rather than replacing them.

**RPwD Rules, 2017, Rule 15.** Rule 15(1) says every establishment shall comply with the listed standards. Clause (a) names the Harmonised Guidelines as the "standard for public buildings": the 2016 Ministry of Urban Development edition at first, replaced by the 2021 MoHUA edition from 5 June 2023 (G.S.R. 413(E)). Clauses (b) to (q) add sector standards, none specific to private housing.

**Court challenge to check.** As far as the project is aware, the Supreme Court in *Rajive Raturi v. Union of India* (November 2024) found Rule 15(1) inconsistent with the Act because it relied on guidelines rather than mandatory standards, and directed the Central Government to frame mandatory rules. Confirm the judgment's directions and whether new rules have been notified since. If they have, they replace level 2 and 3 in this table.

**Harmonised Guidelines 2021.** MoHUA's guidelines cover residential buildings, including apartments, group housing and gated communities (Chapter 6.1), and state that no new built form should be sanctioned for approval or occupancy without incorporating accessibility standards. Rule 15(1)(a) adopts them as the standard "for public buildings" from 5 June 2023.

**UDCPR 2020 (Maharashtra).** Regulation 13.1 contains its own barrier-free provisions, but Regulation 13.1.2 limits them to public buildings on plots larger than 2000 m² and excludes private and public residences. NBC 2016 is named only in Regulation 1.3, as a source for the meaning of undefined words; UDCPR does not adopt NBC's accessibility chapter. The repository's copy is the 2020 text: check later amendments.

**NBC 2016.** Part 3, Clause 13 and Annex B applied accessibility requirements to housing by private developers. As a BIS model code it bound a project only where adopted. Its accessibility chapter was not carried into NBCS 2026.

**NBCS 2026 (SP 7 : 2026).** The fourth revision, renamed the National Building Construction Standards. Its foreword states that it is voluntary and non-binding unless adopted, and that development control rules, including the old Part 3, are left to states. For accessibility it refers to IS 4963 : 2025 as good practice. For example, lifts open to the public are to meet IS 4963 (Part D, Section 5A, 9.4.1) and IS 15330 (9.4.2). The document itself does not state the date on which it replaced NBC 2016; the reported date of 30 April 2026 **needs a BIS notice** to confirm.

**IS 4963 : 2025.** BIS's accessibility standard, aligned with NBC 2016 Annex B and stricter in places (for example, a ramp or lift required above the ground floor, and a 1900 × 1900 mm lift car for new establishments). Its Clause 5 covers housing by private developers. It is written as requirements but, like any BIS standard, binds only where adopted.

## Which instruments cover private residential projects

| Instrument | Common areas of a private apartment project | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| RPwD Act, Section 44 | Arguably yes, for any new structure | Applies the Rule 15 standards to building permission and completion |
| Rule 15(1)(a) | **Depends on whether the project is a "public building"** | Both editions cover housing: the 2016 edition lists residential buildings among public buildings that must comply and asks for access elements in new apartment buildings for rent or sale (Chapter 4); the 2021 edition covers apartments and group housing (Chapter 6.1). Rule 15 adopts them "for public buildings" |
| UDCPR 2020, Regulation 13.1 | No | Residences excluded by 13.1.2 |
| RERA | Indirectly | Through sanctioned plans, specifications and promises |
| NBC 2016, Table 8 | Yes, if adopted | Not adopted by UDCPR for residences |
| NBCS 2026 | Partly | Lifts "open to public"; Part A mentions low-income housing |
| IS 4963 : 2025, Clause 5 | Yes, if adopted | Same residential rule as NBC 2016 Table 8 |

**Needs legal review:** whether the common areas of a private residential project are a "public building" under Section 2(w) for the purposes of Rule 15(1)(a), or are otherwise covered by Section 44's "any structure"; whether that overrides UDCPR's exclusion of residences in Maharashtra; and what follows if a building permission or completion certificate was issued without compliance.

## Which version applies: the date rule

A project is generally assessed against the rules in force when its building permission was granted, unless a later law applies to it expressly. This **needs legal review** for each forum. In practice:

| Building permission granted | Reference standard to cite as good practice | Binding route |
|---|---|---|
| Before 15 June 2017 | NBC 2005 or NBC 2016 (by date) | Earlier law (1995 Act) and state regulations; Rule 15 not yet in force |
| 15 June 2017 to 4 June 2023 | NBC 2016, Part 3, Clause 13 and Annex B | Section 44 with Rule 15(1)(a): Harmonised Guidelines 2016, for public buildings |
| 5 June 2023 to the NBCS 2026 date | NBC 2016, Part 3, Clause 13 and Annex B | Section 44 with Rule 15(1)(a): Harmonised Guidelines 2021, for public buildings |
| After the NBCS 2026 date | IS 4963 : 2025 (through NBCS 2026) | Section 44 with Rule 15(1)(a) as it then stands, subject to the court challenge |

Record the building permission and commencement certificate dates in every case summary so the right row can be chosen. Large projects often have several revised certificates spanning both periods; record each one, as in the Marbella case's [`PROJECT-APPROVALS.md`](../cases/mh/pune-marbella-vs-siddhant/PROJECT-APPROVALS.md).

## How to cite, for contributors and the bot

1. Start from the highest level that applies: the Act, then Rule 15 and its prescribed standard, then the state regulation, then RERA.
2. Use BIS codes for technical detail (dimensions, heights, features), and say how they bind in that case: adopted, prescribed, contractual, or good practice only.
3. Do not describe a BIS clause as mandatory because it says "shall". Name the instrument that makes it binding, or call it good practice.
4. For older projects, cite the version in force at the building permission date, and mention the current standard only for comparison.
5. Flag anything marked **Needs legal review** or **Missing** in this file as unconfirmed.
